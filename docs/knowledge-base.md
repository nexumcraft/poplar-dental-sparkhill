## Treatments

Poplar Dental Practice provides NHS dental care for adults and children. Its NHS.uk listing includes check-ups, fillings, crowns and bridges, gum disease treatment, root canal treatment, scale and polish, tooth extractions, and braces and other orthodontic treatment (listed as a specialist service). Each treatment below has its own page on the website.

### Check-ups and cleaning

**NHS charge band:** Band 1. **Web page:** [check-ups-and-cleaning.html](https://nexumcraft.github.io/poplar-dental-sparkhill/check-ups-and-cleaning)

A regular check-up is the simplest way to keep teeth and gums healthy, and to catch small problems before they become painful.

What happens at a check-up:

- The dentist asks about your general health, any medicines you take, and anything you've noticed in your mouth.
- The dentist examines your teeth, gums and mouth, including checking for early signs of mouth cancer.
- X-rays may be taken if the dentist needs a closer look between or beneath your teeth.
- The dentist explains anything they find and agrees a plan with you before any treatment starts.

**Scale and polish:** if you need it, a scale and polish removes hardened plaque (tartar) that brushing alone can't shift. On the NHS it's part of a Band 1 check-up when it's clinically needed.

**How often to come:** it depends on how healthy your teeth and gums are. The dentist recommends the right gap for you, which can be anywhere from 3 months to 2 years for adults, and up to 12 months for children.

**Children:** bring your child to the dentist as soon as their first milk teeth appear. NHS dental care is free for under-18s, and early visits help children feel relaxed in the dental chair.

### Fillings

**NHS charge band:** Band 2. **Web page:** [fillings.html](https://nexumcraft.github.io/poplar-dental-sparkhill/fillings)

A filling repairs a tooth damaged by decay or a small break, and stops the problem getting worse.

You might need a filling if:

- A tooth is sensitive to hot, cold or sweet food and drink
- You can see or feel a hole, a dark spot or a rough edge
- A small piece of tooth has broken off

Often, decay is found at a check-up before you feel anything at all, which is when it's quickest and simplest to fix.

What happens:

1. The area is numbed with a local anaesthetic so you're comfortable.
2. The dentist removes the decay and cleans the tooth.
3. The tooth is filled, shaped and polished, and the dentist checks your bite feels right.

Most fillings are done in a single visit. The dentist recommends the filling material that's clinically right for your tooth and talks you through the options.

Afterwards: the numbness usually wears off within a few hours, so take care not to bite your cheek or lip in the meantime. A little sensitivity for a few days is normal. If the pain lasts, or your bite feels high, call the practice.

### Crowns and bridges

**NHS charge band:** Band 3. **Web page:** [crowns-and-bridges.html](https://nexumcraft.github.io/poplar-dental-sparkhill/crowns-and-bridges)

A crown protects and strengthens a damaged tooth. A bridge fills the gap left by a missing tooth.

**Crowns:** a crown is a cap that covers the whole visible part of a tooth. The dentist may recommend one if a tooth is badly decayed, broken or heavily filled, or to protect a tooth after root canal treatment.

**Bridges:** a bridge is one or more false teeth held in place by being fixed to the teeth on either side of the gap. It stays in your mouth, so you look after it much like your natural teeth.

What to expect:

1. At the first visit, the dentist prepares the tooth, takes a mould, and fits a temporary crown if you need one.
2. Your crown or bridge is made to fit you by a dental laboratory.
3. At the second visit, the dentist fits it in place and checks your bite.

Looking after them: brush twice a day with a fluoride toothpaste and clean between your teeth every day. Under a bridge, floss threaders or small interdental brushes help. Regular check-ups make sure everything stays healthy.

Band 3 also covers any Band 1 and Band 2 care you need in the same course of treatment.

### Gum disease treatment

**NHS charge band:** Band 1 for simple gum care, Band 2 for more extensive gum treatment. **Web page:** [gum-disease.html](https://nexumcraft.github.io/poplar-dental-sparkhill/gum-disease)

Gum disease is very common and often painless at first. Treated early, it can be stopped before it damages the bone that holds teeth in place.

Signs to look out for:

- Gums that bleed when you brush or floss
- Red, swollen or sore gums
- Bad breath or a bad taste that won't go away
- Gums pulling back from your teeth
- Teeth that feel loose

How the practice treats it:

- Showing you the best way to brush and clean between your teeth at home
- A scale and polish to remove the plaque and tartar that cause inflammation
- If the disease is more advanced, cleaning more deeply below the gum line, using a local anaesthetic if needed
- Checking how your gums are healing at a follow-up visit, and referring you to a specialist if needed

Lowering your risk: brush twice a day with a fluoride toothpaste, clean between your teeth every day, and keep up with check-ups. Smoking and poorly controlled diabetes both raise the risk of gum disease, so stopping smoking and managing blood sugar make a real difference.

### Root canal treatment

**NHS charge band:** Band 2 (Band 3 if a crown is fitted afterwards). **Web page:** [root-canal.html](https://nexumcraft.github.io/poplar-dental-sparkhill/root-canal)

Root canal treatment removes infection from inside a tooth, so you can keep the tooth instead of losing it.

When it's needed: inside each tooth is soft tissue called the pulp. If it becomes infected or inflamed, usually from deep decay, a crack or an injury, root canal treatment can save the tooth. Signs include:

- Pain that lasts, especially when you bite or have hot drinks
- Swelling in your gum or face
- A tooth that has darkened in colour

Sometimes there are no symptoms, and the problem shows up on an X-ray at a check-up.

What happens:

1. The tooth is numbed with a local anaesthetic. It should feel no more uncomfortable than having a filling.
2. The dentist removes the infected pulp, then cleans and shapes the narrow canals inside the root.
3. The canals are filled and sealed. Between visits, the tooth has a temporary filling.

Treatment usually takes two or more visits.

Afterwards: the tooth may feel tender for a few days, and everyday painkillers usually help. Back teeth often need a crown afterwards to protect them. Call the practice if you notice swelling or the pain gets worse.

### Tooth extractions

**NHS charge band:** Band 2. Stopping bleeding after an extraction and removing stitches are free. **Web page:** [tooth-extraction.html](https://nexumcraft.github.io/poplar-dental-sparkhill/tooth-extraction)

The practice always tries to save a tooth. When that isn't possible, it removes the tooth carefully and explains how to look after the area while it heals.

Why a tooth might need removing:

- It's too badly decayed or broken to repair
- Severe gum disease has loosened it
- An infection can't be treated another way
- The mouth is crowded and the dentist advises making space

What happens: the area is numbed with a local anaesthetic first. You may feel some pressure, but you shouldn't feel pain. Afterwards, you bite on a gauze pad to help a blood clot form.

Aftercare for the first 24 hours:

- Don't rinse your mouth, so the blood clot can settle.
- Avoid hot drinks, alcohol and smoking.
- Eat soft food and chew on the other side.
- If it bleeds, bite firmly on a clean, damp gauze or rolled-up handkerchief for 15 minutes.
- After 24 hours, rinse gently with warm salty water after meals.

Call the practice if the bleeding won't stop, the pain gets worse after a few days, or you notice swelling. If the practice is closed, call NHS 111.

### Braces and orthodontics

**NHS charge band:** Band 3, and free for under-18s who qualify. **Web page:** [braces.html](https://nexumcraft.github.io/poplar-dental-sparkhill/braces)

Straighter teeth can be easier to keep clean and more comfortable to bite with. The practice checks whether braces are right for you or your child, and explains the options.

**NHS braces for children:** on the NHS, braces are available for under-18s who have a clear clinical need, for example teeth that are badly crowded or a bite that causes problems. Dentists use a standard scale, the Index of Orthodontic Treatment Need, to decide who qualifies. Treatment that's mainly for appearance isn't usually available on the NHS.

What happens:

1. The dentist checks your child's teeth and bite, usually as part of a routine check-up.
2. If braces could help, the dentist explains what's involved and arranges treatment or a referral to an orthodontic specialist.
3. There may be a waiting list for NHS orthodontic treatment, so it helps to ask early.

**Braces for adults:** NHS braces are rarely available for adults, who usually need to go private. Ask at your next check-up and the dentist will explain what might work for you.

## Urgent and emergency dental care

**Web page:** [urgent-care.html](https://nexumcraft.github.io/poplar-dental-sparkhill/urgent-care)

### Go to A&E or call 999 if you have

- Swelling in your mouth, face or neck that makes it hard to breathe, swallow or talk
- Swelling around your eye that means you can't open it
- Bleeding from your mouth that won't stop
- A serious injury to your face, mouth or jaw

### During opening hours

Call Poplar Dental Practice on 0121 771 3720 as early in the day as you can, from 9am, Monday to Friday. Tell them what's wrong. If they can, they'll offer you an urgent appointment. If not, they'll tell you where else you can be seen.

### When the practice is closed

Use [NHS 111 online](https://111.nhs.uk/triage/check-your-dental-symptoms) or call 111. They can tell you where to get urgent dental care nearby.

### What counts as urgent

- Toothache that painkillers aren't controlling
- Swelling in your gum or face
- A broken, chipped or knocked-out tooth
- Bleeding that won't stop after a tooth has been taken out

### If an adult tooth is knocked out

1. Hold the tooth by the white top part, not the root.
2. If it's dirty, rinse it briefly in milk or cold water.
3. Try to push it back into place, then bite on a clean cloth to hold it.
4. If you can't, keep it in milk or inside your cheek, and see a dentist straight away.

Don't try to put a baby tooth back in.

### Easing pain until you're seen

- Take paracetamol or ibuprofen, following the instructions on the packet. Don't give aspirin to children under 16.
- Avoid food and drink that's very hot, cold or sweet.
- For swelling, hold a cold compress against your cheek.

### Cost of urgent care

Urgent NHS dental treatment costs the same as Band 1, unless you get free NHS dental care.

## NHS dental charges

NHS dental treatment is grouped into charge bands. The practice tells you which band your treatment falls into, and what it will cost, before starting.

| Band | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Band 1 | Examination, diagnosis and advice. Includes X-rays, a scale and polish, and fluoride varnish if clinically needed. |
| Band 2 | Everything in Band 1, plus fillings, root canal treatment, tooth extractions and more extensive gum treatment. |
| Band 3 | Everything in Bands 1 and 2, plus crowns, bridges, dentures and orthodontic treatment such as braces. |
| Urgent | Urgent care to relieve pain or stop a problem getting worse. It costs the same as Band 1. |

- You pay one charge for each course of treatment, even if it takes more than one visit.
- If you need treatment from different bands, you only pay the highest one.
- Charges are set nationally, not by the practice. See the [current NHS dental charges on NHS.uk](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/dentists/dental-costs/how-much-will-i-pay-for-nhs-dental-treatment/).
- For Band 2 or Band 3 treatment, you get a written treatment plan.

## Free NHS dental care

In England, NHS dental treatment is free if, when treatment starts, you are:

- Under 18, or under 19 and in full-time education
- Pregnant, or have had a baby in the last 12 months
- Getting certain benefits, such as income-related Employment and Support Allowance, Pension Credit Guarantee Credit, or Universal Credit below a set income, or you're the partner or dependant of someone who does
- Named on a valid NHS tax credit exemption certificate or HC2 certificate

Bring proof to your appointment. If you claim free treatment you aren't entitled to, you may have to pay a penalty. [Check who can get free NHS dental treatment on NHS.uk](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/dentists/who-can-get-free-nhs-dental-treatment/).

After 24 hours, rinse gently with warm salty water after meals. Call the practice if bleeding won't stop, the pain gets worse after a few days, or you notice swelling.

**Q: Can my child get braces on the NHS?**
A: NHS braces are available for under-18s with a clear clinical need, assessed using the Index of Orthodontic Treatment Need. The dentist checks your child's teeth and bite and arranges treatment or a referral to a specialist if braces could help. There may be a waiting list.

**Q: Can adults get braces?**
A: NHS braces are rarely available for adults, who usually need to go private. Ask the dentist at your next check-up.

**Q: My gums bleed when I brush. Should I see a dentist?**
A: Bleeding gums can be a sign of gum disease, which is very common and treatable, especially early. Book a check-up with the practice so a dentist can take a look.
