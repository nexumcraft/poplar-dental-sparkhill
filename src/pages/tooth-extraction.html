Tooth extractions

We'll always try to save a tooth. When that isn't possible, we remove it carefully and explain how to look after the area while it heals.

Why a tooth might need removing

  • It's too badly decayed or broken to repair
  • Severe gum disease has loosened it
  • An infection can't be treated another way
  • Your mouth is crowded and the dentist advises making space

What happens

We numb the area with a local anaesthetic first. You may feel some pressure, but you shouldn't feel pain. Afterwards, we'll ask you to bite on a gauze pad to help a blood clot form.

Aftercare for the first 24 hours

  • Don't rinse your mouth, so the blood clot can settle.
  • Avoid hot drinks, alcohol and smoking.
  • Eat soft food and chew on the other side.
  • If it bleeds, bite firmly on a clean, damp gauze or rolled-up handkerchief for 15 minutes.
  • After 24 hours, rinse gently with warm salty water after meals.

Call us if the bleeding won't stop, the pain gets worse after a few days, or you notice swelling. If we're closed, call NHS 111.
